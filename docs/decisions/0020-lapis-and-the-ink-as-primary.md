# 0020. Black Iris and Lapis: the ink is the primary, the blue is an ink and a surface, signal colours are derived

- Status: accepted — supersedes [0019](0019-two-colour-design-tokens.md)
- Date: 2026-09-25

## Context and Problem Statement

Phase 3a shipped the two-colour tokens of [ADR-0019](0019-two-colour-design-tokens.md) with Frozen `#A0BDDB` in two roles: the primary fill in both themes — 1.87:1 on paper, so every primary control carried a 1 px `--input` edge to be seen at all — and the dark theme's ink. The definition of done's second gate is a human look at `/design` on real screens, not a contrast table, and on 2026-09-25 Saman rejected both roles: a paragraph of Frozen on Iris reads too blue, and a pale rectangle as the add-to-cart button does not work for a store. ADR-0019 had also left every state colour beyond the destructive red — in stock, low stock, a discount — to "a new ADR and new derivations, not a class", and the catalogue slice (3b) needs them. The question is what fills the primary button, what the second brand colour is for if Frozen can never be an ink, and how three state colours enter a system whose rule is that no colour is typed twice.

Web research on 2026-09-25 (stores fetched directly where reachable; the report marks every value measured or claimed): every store with data uses a white page — 9 of 9 global stores, 5 of 5 Iranian marketplaces with a published value, 3 of 3 Iranian supplement stores measured — and 8 of 9 global stores set body text in a near-black between #030303 and #333333 rather than pure black. The add-to-cart fill has no dominant hue: near-black at Nike, Etsy and Zara and in Shopify's default theme (#121212), blue at Apple (#0071E3), eBay (#0968F6) and Walmart, yellow at Amazon (#FFD814), red at Target; the label is white on every dark or saturated fill. Iranian marketplaces are red-branded, 6 of 9 (Digikala #ED1944, Divar, Basalam, Okala, Emalls, Torob) against two blues and one orange; Digikala's offer badge is red (#E6123D), its low-stock note is red text with an exact count (#D32F2F), its trust signals are green (#00A049), and its own guideline treats white as a primary colour and reserves black backgrounds for campaigns. Where a sale colour could be read it is red at 5 of 11 stores; in stock is green at 5 of 5 global stores that have a success token; the old price is grey and struck through at 3 of 3 measured; two Iranian supplement stores colour the discounted price itself green. Baymard, Nielsen Norman Group and WCAG ask for a button that stands out and meets contrast (text 4.5:1, control 3:1 against its surroundings), not for a hue; HubSpot's often-quoted 21 % lift for red was a red button on a green page. All five Iranian supplement stores lead with authenticity badges. About 4.7 % of Iranian men are colour-blind, so red and green states carry text or an icon, never colour alone.

Two palettes were then applied to a mock storefront and shown to Saman side by side. Palette A kept Frozen as a surface-only second colour under the ink as the primary: the tonal secondary button, the tints, the selection highlight, never text. Palette B replaced Frozen with Lapis `#1762B6`, a saturated blue that is an ink at 5.82:1 on paper and so can also be the link, the focus ring and the label of its own tonal button. He chose B on 2026-09-25, and with it a single light theme ([ADR-0021](0021-a-single-light-theme.md)).

## Considered Options

1. **Keep Frozen as the primary fill and only lighten the dark ink** — the one-token remedy §5.3 of the 3a spec had planned for a too-blue paragraph. It leaves the 1.87:1 add-to-cart button and its edge exactly as rejected, and still has no colour for stock or a discount.
2. **The ink as the primary, Frozen kept as a surface-only second colour** — the first proposal, previewed as palette A. It keeps the colour from the original brand reference, but a blue that can never carry text leaves every link, the focus ring and every label in the ink, so the second brand colour is decoration and nothing else.
3. **The ink as the primary, with a saturated blue that can be an ink, a link and a ring** — palette B. Lapis, oklch(0.50 0.15 255), is derived by the same rule as the signal inks and is measured in every role it takes: the link at 5.82:1 on paper and 6.07:1 on the card, the focus ring at 5.82:1 over the page, the label of the tonal secondary button (Lapis 12 % over paper) at 4.91:1 and of its hover (the 16 % accent tint) at 4.63:1, and the selection background under the ink at 15.18:1; never a fill under white text. Chosen by Saman after seeing both palettes applied to the mock store.
4. **An orange sale chip** — palette C. Rejected: as a shape it fails 3:1 against a white card, and it departs from the Iranian red convention for discounts.

Under every option, `destructive`, `success` and `warning` are derived from OKLCH triples, each with a `-foreground` for solid fills and a `-soft` surface for state, and `sale` equals `destructive`.

## Decision Outcome

Chosen: **option 3**, because it is the only one where the second brand colour earns its place: it answers the three questions without a third colour and without a blue that exists only as decoration. The ink as the fill is what the near-black buttons of Nike, Zara and Shopify's default theme do, and it is the strongest element a page can carry — 19.10:1 under a paper label, with no edge. Lapis is an ink and a surface: the link, the focus ring, the tonal secondary button's label on its own 12 % tint, the 16 % accent tint that the secondary and the ghost button hover to, and the selection highlight — and never a fill under white text. The rule for Lapis and the signal inks: hue per colour (red 28, green 150, amber 65, blue 255); lightness 0.50; chroma the largest hundredth at least 0.01 below the sRGB gamut boundary for that lightness and hue — the boundary being the largest chroma whose linear-light sRGB channels all stay within 0 to 1, found by bisection — capped at 0.18; which yields red 0.19, capped to 0.18, green 0.12, amber 0.10 and blue 0.15. The gamut-limited green and amber are why an AA amber ink is brown: at L 0.50 sRGB has no orange left. `--sale` equals `--destructive` on purpose — red is the Iranian discount convention (Digikala, Divar), and a view never carries two reds. Solid fills carry a `-foreground`; state badges and notices use a `-soft` surface, the ink pre-mixed at 12 % over the card, under the ink itself. `apps/web/test/tokens.test.ts` re-derives Lapis and every signal ink from its OKLCH triple (`oklchToHex`, checked against Tailwind 4's published palette), every tint from Lapis or Iris over paper, every soft surface from its ink, and measures every text pair at ≥ 4.5:1 and the ring and edges at ≥ 3:1; the CSS stays hex, and only the test computes OKLCH. The mechanism of ADR-0019 — derived tokens under shadcn's names, the deleted default palette, the tokens test — carries forward unchanged.

### Consequences

- Good: one blue does link, ring, tonal button, highlight and selection, and each role is measured — 5.82:1, 5.82:1, 4.91:1, 4.63:1 and 15.18:1.
- Good: every chromatic ink sits at OKLCH lightness 0.50, so red, green, amber and blue read as one family.
- Good: the call to action is the strongest element on every page and reads as the near-black convention of premium and fitness stores.
- Bad: a more conventional look than the pastel — the trade Saman accepted.
- Bad: Frozen, the colour from the original brand reference, leaves the system.
- Bad: an AA amber ink is brown, by physics: at L 0.50 there is no orange inside sRGB.

**Reversed if** a brand identity arrives with its own colours, or a real test shows the red sale chip underperforming.
